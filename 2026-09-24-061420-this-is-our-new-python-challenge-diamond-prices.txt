● Step 6 done and pushed. Suite at 50 passing (16s).

  The step's premise turned out to be wrong

  The plan assumed near-collinear x/y/z (r≥0.97) would destabilize OLS and that
  regularization would rescue it. It doesn't, and I tested it directly rather
  than assuming:

  ┌───────┬───────────────────┬──────────┐
  │ model │ best variant RMSE │ R² (log) │
  ├───────┼───────────────────┼──────────┤
  │ OLS   │ $773.85           │ 0.9829   │
  ├───────┼───────────────────┼──────────┤
  │ Ridge │ $773.86           │ 0.9829   │
  ├───────┼───────────────────┼──────────┤
  │ Lasso │ $823.84           │ 0.9821   │
  └───────┴───────────────────┴──────────┘

  Ridge reproduces OLS to four decimals on every variant, and its predictions
  agree within 0.01 log units. Cross-validation picks small alphas (0.1–1.0).

  The direct evidence: I refit both on 10 bootstrap resamples of the training
  set and compared coefficient stability on the collinear x/y/z variant. OLS
  standard deviations were x 0.048 / y 0.061 / z 0.103 — no worse than ridge's
  0.052 / 0.062 / 0.133. Collinearity does inflate variance in principle, but
  with n=43,012 against at most 22 features there's plenty of information left
  to separate the features. Lasso is actively slightly worse: with no excess
  variance to trade away, shrinkage only costs signal.

  So the implication for step 10 is to prefer the simpler OLS. I've written this
  into PLAN.md rather than quietly leaving the stale premise in place.

  Two things worth noting

  Lasso zeroes out table on the carat variant — independent agreement with the
  EDA finding that table carries almost no price information.
